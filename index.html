How it works

From a page you read to an episode you hear

One Wikipedia article, followed through the whole pipeline. The script lines and the audio below are from the real episode.

  1. 01

    Bring a source

    A link, a PDF, a screenshot or your own notes. Vozonda reads it once and keeps what matters, up to ten sources per episode.

  2. 02

    Read the script first

    Your local model writes the conversation, in one of fourteen show formats. You read every line and fix what is wrong before anything is voiced.

  3. 03

    Voices record it, with the engine you pick

    One to three voices, or a single narrator. Voice engines are plugins: Kokoro and Piper on your CPU, Qwen3-TTS on a GPU, Voxtral as a hosted option, more on the way. This sample is one engine; switching is a setting, and so is the language model that writes the script.

  4. 04

    It lands in your own feed

    A private RSS feed with chapters and transcripts. Apple Podcasts, Pocket Casts, AntennaPod: it just plays. Publishing to Nostr is optional.

FAQ

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. The software is MIT-licensed and complete. The cloud, if it comes, is for people who would rather not run a model server; it will not get features the open-source version lacks.

What hardware do I need?

For the default setup, a recent computer with about 16 GB RAM: voices run on the CPU and a small local model writes the script. An NVIDIA GPU gives you the studio voices and bigger models.

Can I trust what the hosts say?

The script is written from your sources only and you can read and fix it before it is voiced. Like any language model it can still get a detail wrong, which is exactly why the review step exists.

Which podcast apps work?

All of them. Vozonda writes a standard RSS feed with Podcasting 2.0 chapters and transcripts. Keep it private with a feed key, or publish it.

How does the blind test work?

You hear the same minute of the same source from both tools, in random order and at the same loudness, and pick one. Results and raw votes are public and update every five minutes.
